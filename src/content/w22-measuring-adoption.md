# Measuring AI Adoption and Productivity Improvement

## Why adoption metrics matter

"The team is using Claude" is not a success metric. A team can use Claude daily, on low-value tasks, in ways that create more work through verification than they save through generation. Measuring adoption without measuring value gives you a false picture.

The metrics that matter answer two questions: Is the team using AI in the intended ways? And is it producing measurable value?

## Adoption metrics

**Usage breadth** — how many analysts are actively using Claude for work tasks, not just experimenting. Define a threshold: "used Claude at least 3 times this week for a support task" is active. Set a target for 30, 60, and 90 days.

**Use case coverage** — are analysts using AI for the use cases you trained them on, or self-discovering new ones? Both are good, but the former means training is working and the latter means you need updated guidance.

**Prompt quality improvement** — do analysts' prompts get more specific over time? This is harder to measure systematically but observable in the prompts you see being shared or asked about.

**Safety compliance rate** — what percentage of AI interactions follow your data rules? This is measurable if you establish a self-reporting mechanism or spot-check in team reviews.

## Value metrics

**Time saved per use case** — the most direct measure. Before rollout: record baseline time for 3–5 specific tasks (ops report drafting, 1:1 prep, KB article creation). After rollout: record time with AI. The difference is your measured value.

**Quality improvement** — QA scores before and after AI adoption for ticket resolutions, communications, and documentation. If analysts are using AI well, quality should be stable or improving, not declining.

**Volume handled without staffing increase** — if ticket volume grows 15% and the team handles it without additional headcount, AI-assisted productivity is part of the explanation. Isolate this from other factors carefully before claiming causation.

**Error rate change** — measure the errors that AI is supposed to reduce (miscategorized tickets, incomplete PIRs, missing action item owners). Before-and-after comparison with consistent measurement methodology.

## Setting up your measurement baseline

The most common mistake: trying to measure value without a before-measurement. Once AI is deployed, you cannot reconstruct what the baseline was.

Before Phase 1 rollout, measure and record:
- Time for your weekly ops report (from raw data to finished document)
- Time for a typical 1:1 prep session
- Ticket quality score baseline (from your existing QA process)
- Volume of KB articles created per month

These four numbers, tracked after rollout, tell you whether AI deployment delivered measurable value.
